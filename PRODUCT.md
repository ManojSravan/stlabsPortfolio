# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Founders and small teams hiring a product engineer — from early-stage builders who need an MVP shipped to funded founders scaling an existing product. They arrive with a product idea or a stuck project, often comparing studios and freelancers, and they need to quickly judge whether Manoj is someone they can trust with production work.

## Product Purpose

Sravan Tech Labs is a product engineering studio portfolio for Manoj Sravan. The site exists to show how he designs, builds, and ships real software for founders — and to turn that credibility into conversations. Success looks like a visitor understanding the studio's craft (case studies, writing, founder story), feeling confident in direct collaboration without agency overhead, and booking a call or reaching out.

## Brand Personality

Warm, personal, trusted. Founder-to-founder tone: clear and capable without corporate polish or hype. The voice should feel like one builder speaking directly to another — honest about scope, confident in craft, approachable in collaboration.

## Anti-references

Generic agency marketing sites: purple gradients, stock handshake photos, carousel heroes, and hollow claims like "we innovate" or "digital transformation." Anything that reads like a template studio rather than a specific person with a point of view.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell** — Lead with shipped work, case studies, and concrete outcomes instead of abstract service claims.
2. **Founder-to-founder** — Write and design as one person talking to another builder, not a faceless agency.
3. **Direct path to trust** — Every section should earn credibility (proof, clarity, craft) before asking for conversion.
4. **Craft over clutter** — Boutique studio restraint: strong typography, intentional spacing, no decorative noise.
5. **Practice what you preach** — The portfolio itself should feel like production-quality product engineering.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best-effort accessibility: semantic structure, readable contrast, and respect for `prefers-reduced-motion` where animations are used. No formal WCAG certification target yet, but avoid known barriers (unlabeled controls, low-contrast body text, motion-only information).
